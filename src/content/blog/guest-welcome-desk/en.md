---
title: "Setting up an event welcome desk that avoids queues"
description: "How to lay out and staff an event welcome desk: guest list format, badge pick-up, late arrivals and the questions guests always ask."
publishedAt: 2026-11-24
---

Guests form their first impression of an event in the five minutes at the welcome desk. A queue in the rain or a missing name sets the tone for the morning. Most queues come from three causes: one list for everyone, one person doing everything, and questions the desk cannot answer.

## Split the flow before it reaches the desk

- **Several lines by surname** (A–F, G–M, N–Z) for groups above about 80 people, with large signs readable from the entrance.
- **A separate point for speakers, sponsors and press**, so they do not wait behind guests.
- **A "problems" point** a few metres away for missing names, payment questions and changes. The main lines keep moving.

## Choose a guest list format you can search fast

A printed list sorted by surname is the most reliable: it works without Wi-Fi and without battery. If you use a check-in app, keep a printed copy anyway, and test the app on the venue's network the day before.

Print badges in advance and sort them in the same order as the list. Keep blank badges and a marker for late registrations.

## Staff the desk properly

Count one person per 40 to 60 guests arriving in the main half-hour, plus one floater who handles the problems point. Brief them on:

- the programme and where each room is,
- the location of cloakroom, toilets, Wi-Fi code and accessible entrance,
- the [photo opt-out signal](/en/blog/photo-consent-at-events), so they can hand out the right lanyard,
- who to call for anything they cannot answer.

## Handle late arrivals

After the opening, reduce the desk to one person and move the badges for absent guests to a single box. Put a sign with the programme and the current session so late guests can join without interrupting.

## Close the loop

At the end of the morning, count the badges that were not collected. That gives your real attendance, which you will need for the caterer, the sponsors and next year's planning. Add it to your [event checklist](/en/blog/event-day-checklist).
