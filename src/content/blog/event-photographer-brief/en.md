---
title: "How to brief an event photographer: a one-page template"
description: "What to tell your event photographer before the day: schedule, must-have shots, people to find, delivery format and consent rules, on one page."
publishedAt: 2026-11-24
---

A photographer who arrives with a clear brief spends the day taking pictures instead of asking questions. The brief does not need to be long. One page, sent a week before the event and reread together on the morning, covers almost everything.

## 1. The practical frame

Start with what the photographer needs to get in and get started:

- **Address, access and parking**, including the entrance for suppliers if it differs from the guest entrance.
- **Arrival time** and the name and phone number of the person who will meet them.
- **Coverage hours**, with any break you have agreed on.
- **Dress code**, so they blend in with your staff or your guests.
- **Where to leave equipment**, and whether that room locks.

## 2. The running order

Paste the timed programme, then mark the moments that cannot be missed. A photographer can only be in one place at a time: if two key moments overlap, say which one wins.

| Time | Moment | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 09:00 | Guests arrive, welcome desk | Medium |
| 09:30 | Opening speech | High |
| 11:00 | Panel discussion | Medium |
| 12:30 | Group photo on the steps | High |
| 18:00 | Awards | High |

## 3. The shot list

Split it into three groups so the photographer knows what to protect when time runs short:

1. **Must-have**: the group photo, every speaker on stage, sponsors' booths, the venue before guests arrive.
2. **Nice to have**: candid networking, details (badges, food, signage), the team at work.
3. **Only if time allows**: portraits on request, behind the scenes.

## 4. The people to find

List the people who must appear, with a way to recognise them: a photo, a role, a badge colour. Name one staff member who can point them out on the day. This single list prevents the most common disappointment: the CEO or the bride's grandmother missing from every picture.

## 5. Consent and the people who opt out

Tell the photographer how guests signal that they do not want to be photographed (a lanyard colour, a sticker) and what to do if someone asks to have a picture deleted. If minors attend, say so explicitly. Our [guide to photo consent at events](/en/blog/photo-consent-at-events) explains how to set this up.

## 6. Delivery

Agree on these points in writing before the day:

- **Delivery date** for a first selection and for the full set.
- **Format and size**: high resolution for print, web-sized for social media.
- **Number of edited photos** you can expect, as a range rather than a promise.
- **Usage rights**: where you may publish the photos and whether sponsors may reuse them.

## The morning check

Fifteen minutes with the photographer on arrival is enough: walk the venue, confirm the must-have moments, introduce the staff contact. Keep the one-page brief printed in your pocket; it is the reference when the schedule slips.
