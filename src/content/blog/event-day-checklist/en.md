Most event problems come from a task nobody owned. This checklist splits the work by time so each item has a deadline and, ideally, a name next to it. Adapt the timing to your event: a 30-person workshop needs less lead time than a 500-person conference.

## Four weeks before

- [ ] Confirm the venue in writing: capacity, opening hours, accessibility, Wi-Fi, power outlets.
- [ ] Book the key suppliers: catering, photographer, audiovisual, cleaning if not included.
- [ ] Publish the registration page with the programme, the address and the photo policy.
- [ ] Draft the running order with timings.
- [ ] Decide who owns each area on the day: welcome, stage, catering, speakers.

## Two weeks before

- [ ] Send the [photographer brief](/en/blog/event-photographer-brief) and the running order to suppliers.
- [ ] Collect speakers' slides and check they open on the venue's equipment.
- [ ] Order badges, signage and printed material.
- [ ] Plan the [welcome desk](/en/blog/guest-welcome-desk): layout, staff, guest list format.
- [ ] Prepare a plan B for outdoor moments.

## The week before

- [ ] Send guests a practical reminder: time, address, access, what to bring.
- [ ] Confirm final numbers with the caterer.
- [ ] Share one contact sheet with every supplier and staff member's phone number.
- [ ] Print the running order, the guest list and the contact sheet.
- [ ] Pack a kit: tape, scissors, markers, extension cords, chargers, first-aid kit, spare badges.

## On the day

- [ ] Arrive before suppliers to open the venue and check the room setup.
- [ ] Test microphones, projector and slides in the real room.
- [ ] Brief the staff in ten minutes: roles, timings, who decides if things slip.
- [ ] Walk the venue with the photographer.
- [ ] Keep one person free of fixed tasks to handle the unexpected.

## The day after

- [ ] Thank speakers, suppliers and volunteers.
- [ ] Send guests a short message with the next steps and, when ready, the photos.
- [ ] Note what went wrong while it is fresh, with the fix for next time.
- [ ] Pay suppliers and close the budget.

Print this list, give every line an owner, and review it in the weekly team meeting. A ticked box is only useful if everyone agrees on what "done" means.
